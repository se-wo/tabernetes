### Items

1. **The decision for UDP** (FL6). Add `func (p *netPolicy) allowedUDP(ctx context.Context, src, dst netip.AddrPort) bool` next to `allowed` (`tabnode_netpol.go`, line 124). It does what `allowed` does, with `Proto: v1.ProtocolUDP` in the `network.Packet`. You can make one function with the protocol as an argument for both; `allowed` keeps its signature for the TCP callers.
   - Until the informers have synced, allow everything, as `allowed` does.
   - The host of the node is not examined, as `admitIngress` does for TCP: a source in `127.0.0.0/8`, the node IP (`tabNodeIP`), and a datagram of a pod to its own pod IP.
2. **The hook.** In an `init()` of `tabnode_netpol.go`, set `udpPolicy` to a function that calls `theTabNode.policy.allowedUDP`. With no tab node or no policy (`theTabNode == nil`), it allows. The engine evaluates the egress of the source and the ingress of the destination in one call, as for TCP.
3. **FL7.** One flow causes one call of the engine for its first datagram, and no call for the next datagrams and the answers. Count the calls in a test (wrap `udpPolicy` or the engine).
4. **The strict mode for flows** (FL8, FL9; `tabnode_netpol_strict.go`). `recheck` closes the TCP connections that the policy denies now. Also call `theTabNode.net.recheckFlows(...)` there, with a function that calls `p.allowedUDP`. The delay is the same (`recheckDelay`, 200 ms, in `watchChanges`). After the recheck, the next datagram of a removed flow is a new flow. It gets a new decision, and a denied datagram does not arrive.
5. **FL12.** A datagram that the policy denies makes no flow. When a new policy allows it, the next datagram arrives at once, not after 30 seconds. W4.2a makes no flow for false; you test it with real policies.
6. **Section 10.3** is for W4.5b. Send the names of your unit tests in your report. The e2e tests of FL8, FL9 and FL12 are W4.5b.
