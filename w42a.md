The path of a datagram in `(*udpConn).WriteTo` and `Write`:

1. A datagram of more than 65,507 bytes gives `errDatagramTooLarge` (EMSGSIZE). This check comes before the pending error.
2. Then the closed state, a past write deadline and the pending error.
3. A dst in 127.0.0.0/8 becomes the socket's IP.
4. `udpRoute(c.ctx, src, dst)` returns `from` and `to`, or `udpRefuse` (refused at once) or `udpDrop`. It is called without a lock of the table, and it must not wait. A test can swap it (`udpHook`).
5. If no socket has `to` and the IP is a pod IP of this tab node or `tabNodeIP`, the datagram is refused (UD8). For any other IP, it is dropped (phase 5). If the receiver has a "udp" loopback row and the real `src` IP is not the receiver's IP, it is refused (UD21).
6. In `deliver`, a connected receiver drops a datagram when `from != remote`. A full queue (256 KiB of payload) drops the datagram.

The pending error:

- A refusal sets it. One of ReadFrom, Read, WriteTo or Write takes it, whichever comes first.
- The call returns an error that wraps `syscall.ECONNREFUSED` (n=0). A write that takes it sends nothing.
- Unconnected sockets never get it.
- The constants `udpPendingOnRead`, `udpPendingOnWrite`, `udpPendingWriteSends` and `udpRefuseUnconnected` hold the runwasi points.

Other parts of the API:

- `Connect(netip.AddrPort{})` and `Connect(0.0.0.0:0)` both remove the remote address.
- `Write` without a remote address gives EDESTADDRREQ.
- `listenPodUDP` errors wrap `errPortInUse` (binds are exclusive) or `EADDRNOTAVAIL`. Port 0 uses `claimFreePort(proto, ip)` in `tabnode_dial.go`.
- `ReadFrom` gives the real table IPs. The pod worker maps the pod IP to 127.0.0.1 for loopback sockets.
- `Close` and the end of ctx remove the socket, its port and its loopback row.
