- Facts from phase 2:
  - The context of the container is in `p.State(wasiCtxState, ...)`, set by `startComponent` (`tabnode_wasi.go`). Read it with `wasiPeerCtx(p)` (`tabnode_wasi_fetch.go`). It has the `procEnv` of the pod: `procOf(ctx).ip` is the pod IP. `dialCluster`, `listenPod` and `acctFrom(ctx)` use it.
  - `synTimeout` (`tabnode_netpol.go`) is a variable. `setDurations` (`tabnode_wasi_fetch_test.go`) makes it short in a test.
  - `newComponentClient` and `dialComponent` exist in `tabnode_dial.go`.
  - All pods and nodes of one tab share approximately 125 Wasm memories (section 14). Follow-up W2.4 works on this. Each running command component uses 1. Use few component pods, share them between tests, and delete them.
  - Deleting an emulated busybox pod does not stop its background jobs. Use clients that end on their own: `wget -T`, `nc -w`.
  - `evalOn` (`web/test/e2e/devtools.mjs`) reaches Go threads only, not pod workers.
  - The `busybox`, `wget` and `curl` of the tab node are Go emulations (`tabnode_images.go`, `coreutils.go`). Use raw `nc` for checks of the protocol (`coreutils_misc.go`). At the end of its stdin, `nc` closes its send direction (half-close). `nc` and `wget` change `localhost` to the pod IP before they dial (`resolveName`).
- The message contract:
  - The socket messages are `bind`, `bound`, `listen`, `listening`, `accepted`, `connect`, `connected`, `data`, `credit`, `error`, `shutdown`, `closed`, `lookup` and `addresses`.
  - Limits (`LIMITS`, `wasiLimits`): `data` up to 256 KiB, `credit` up to 256 KiB, a backlog of 1 to 4096, a hostname of 1 to 253 characters, and 256 addresses.
  - The ID suffixes do the check of MS2 in the dispatcher: `bind` and `connect` have `socket~`, `accepted` has `socket+`, `closed` has `socket-`, `lookup` has `lookup+` and `addresses` has `lookup-`.
  - `apply` claims the ID of `bind`/`connect` when the message passes the examination. The ID stays owned after `bound` or `connected` with an error code, until `closed` (rule 9).
  - For MS6, the Go dispatcher sends `closed` with `invalid-argument` (`wasiCloseMsg`), also for an ID that is not owned, and runs the `wasiOnClose` functions.
  - Go handlers:
    - `wasiHandle(type, fn)` and `wasiOnClose(kind, fn)` in an `init()`;
    - `p.send`, `p.NewID("socket")`, `p.State(key, mk)`, and `m.Bytes`/`m.Uint`/`m.Str`;
    - a handler runs on the event loop and must not block;
    - `p.send` from a goroutine is safe (`tabnode_wasi_fetch.go`);
    - `acctFrom` takes the lock of the pod, so call it off the event loop.
  - Pod worker: `createPeer`, `peer.on(type, fn)` (one handler for each type), `peer.send(type, fields, transfer)`.
- The network of a tab node (`net_fake.go` of Go 1.27.3, `tabnode_dial.go`, `tabnode_netpol.go`):
  - `listenPod` changes `""`, `0.0.0.0`, `::`, `localhost`, `127.0.0.1` and `::1` to the pod IP. In a pod, it adds `policyListener` for ingress.
  - A dial without `LocalAddr` has the source `127.0.0.1`. The kubelet probes (`tabnode_probe.go`), the lifecycle hooks (`tabnode_lifecycle.go`) and port-forward (`tabStreamer.PortForward`, `tabnode_server.go`) dial in this way. `admitIngress` allows a loopback source.
  - For port 0, the in-process network gives ports from a counter that starts at 1, for all IPs together (fact 11).
  - A fake listener puts connections into its queue before `Accept`, up to its backlog. A dial to a full queue waits until its context ends, then gives `ETIMEDOUT`.
  - Half-close: `*net.TCPConn`, `trackedConn` (strict mode) and `streamConn` (other nodes) have `CloseWrite`. `trackedConn` has no `CloseRead`.
